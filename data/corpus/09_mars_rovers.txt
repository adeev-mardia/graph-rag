NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory in Pasadena, California, has led most of NASA's robotic exploration of Mars, including the Mars rovers Sojourner, Spirit, Opportunity, Curiosity, and Perseverance. The Perseverance rover landed in Jezero Crater on Mars in February 2021 and carried a small helicopter named Ingenuity, which became the first aircraft to achieve powered flight on another planet. The Jet Propulsion Laboratory also managed the Viking program in the 1970s, which sent the first American spacecraft to successfully land on Mars and return images from its surface. Data gathered by the Curiosity rover, which landed in 2012, provided evidence that ancient Mars once had conditions suitable for microbial life, informing NASA's later decision to send Perseverance to search for biosignatures. NASA has described the Perseverance rover's eventual sample-return mission as a precursor to the kind of crewed Mars missions that SpaceX's Starship program is designed to support. The Jet Propulsion Laboratory is operated by the California Institute of Technology under contract to NASA.
