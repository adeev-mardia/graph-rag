The European Space Agency launched the Rosetta spacecraft in 2004 on a mission to study comet 67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko, finally arriving at the comet in 2014 after a ten-year journey through the solar system. Rosetta deployed a small lander named Philae, which made the first soft landing on a comet's surface in November 2014, though Philae's harpoons failed to anchor it properly and it bounced into a shadowed location. The Cassini-Huygens mission, a joint effort between NASA, the European Space Agency, and the Italian Space Agency, launched in 1997 to study Saturn and its moons. The Huygens probe, built by the European Space Agency, separated from the Cassini orbiter and landed on Saturn's moon Titan in January 2005, becoming the most distant landing ever achieved by a human-made spacecraft at that time. NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory managed the Cassini orbiter itself, connecting the Cassini-Huygens mission to the same laboratory that built the Voyager probes and the Mars rovers. The European Space Agency's experience landing Philae on a comet directly informed engineering decisions made during the Huygens landing on Titan.
