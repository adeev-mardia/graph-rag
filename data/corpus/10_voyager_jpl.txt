The Jet Propulsion Laboratory also designed and operated the Voyager 1 and Voyager 2 probes, which launched in 1977 to take advantage of a rare planetary alignment that allowed a single mission to visit Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune. Voyager 2 remains the only spacecraft to have flown by both Uranus and Neptune, sending back the first close-up images of those two outer planets. Voyager 1 crossed into interstellar space in 2012, becoming the first human-made object to leave the heliosphere, the bubble of charged particles surrounding the Sun. Both Voyager probes carry a Golden Record containing sounds and images meant to represent life on Earth to any intelligent species that might one day encounter the spacecraft. The Voyager missions were managed by the same Jet Propulsion Laboratory that later built the Mars rovers Curiosity and Perseverance, and many of the engineering techniques used for deep-space navigation on Voyager carried forward into the Mars rover missions.
