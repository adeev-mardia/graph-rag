NASA's Artemis program, named after the Greek goddess who was the twin sister of Apollo, aims to return astronauts to the Moon for the first time since Apollo 17 in 1972. The Artemis program uses the Space Launch System rocket, developed partly using engine technology inherited from the Space Shuttle program, alongside the Orion crew capsule. Artemis I launched uncrewed in November 2022 to test the Space Launch System and Orion capsule on a journey around the Moon. NASA selected SpaceX's Starship vehicle to serve as the human landing system for Artemis III, linking Elon Musk's company directly to NASA's lunar ambitions. The Artemis program also depends on the Gateway, a planned small space station in lunar orbit being developed with the European Space Agency and other international partners, echoing the international cooperation first established on the International Space Station. NASA has said that lessons learned from the Apollo program's lunar module design influenced early concepts for the Artemis lunar lander requirements.
