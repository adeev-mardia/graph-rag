Elon Musk founded SpaceX, formally Space Exploration Technologies Corporation, in 2002 with the long-term goal of making human life on Mars possible. SpaceX developed the Falcon 9 rocket, which became the first orbital-class rocket capable of landing its first stage for reuse, achieving this milestone in December 2015. The reusability of the Falcon 9 dramatically lowered the cost of access to orbit compared to earlier expendable rockets like the Saturn V. In 2020, SpaceX's Crew Dragon capsule carried NASA astronauts to the International Space Station, marking the first crewed orbital launch from American soil since the Space Shuttle program ended in 2011. SpaceX has also worked closely with NASA under the Commercial Crew Program, the same agency that once relied solely on its own Saturn V and Space Shuttle vehicles. Musk has stated that SpaceX's Starship vehicle, still in development, is intended to support NASA's Artemis program and eventual crewed missions to Mars. SpaceX's rise represented a shift of American human spaceflight from a purely government-run program toward public-private partnership.
