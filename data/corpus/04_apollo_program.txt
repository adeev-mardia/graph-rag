The Apollo program was NASA's effort to land astronauts on the Moon, launched in response to President John F. Kennedy's 1961 challenge to Congress. The program relied on the Saturn V rocket, a massive launch vehicle designed under the leadership of Wernher von Braun at the Marshall Space Flight Center in Huntsville, Alabama. Von Braun had previously developed the V-2 rocket for Germany during the Second World War before moving to the United States to work for the US Army and later NASA. The Apollo program suffered a major setback in 1967 when a cabin fire during a launch pad test killed astronauts Gus Grissom, Ed White, and Roger Chaffee aboard Apollo 1. NASA redesigned the Apollo command module after the Apollo 1 fire before resuming crewed missions. Apollo 8, crewed by Jim Lovell, Frank Borman, and William Anders, became the first crewed spacecraft to orbit the Moon in December 1968. The success of Apollo 8 set the stage for the first crewed lunar landing attempt the following year.
