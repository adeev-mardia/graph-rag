The Hubble Space Telescope was launched into low Earth orbit in 1990 aboard the Space Shuttle Discovery, becoming one of the most productive scientific instruments NASA has ever operated. Hubble's initial images were blurry due to a flaw in its primary mirror, which astronauts repaired during a Space Shuttle servicing mission in 1993. The Hubble Space Telescope was developed jointly by NASA and the European Space Agency, the same European agency that later partnered with NASA on the International Space Station and the Artemis program's Gateway station. After decades of observations, Hubble was succeeded by the James Webb Space Telescope, which launched in December 2021 and observes primarily in infrared light rather than the visible light that Hubble specializes in. The James Webb Space Telescope was also developed through a partnership between NASA, the European Space Agency, and the Canadian Space Agency, the same Canadian agency that contributes to the International Space Station. Engineers who had worked on Hubble's servicing missions at the Goddard Space Flight Center contributed design expertise to the James Webb Space Telescope project.
