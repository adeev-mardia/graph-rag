Apollo 11 launched from the Kennedy Space Center on July 16, 1969, carrying astronauts Neil Armstrong, Buzz Aldrin, and Michael Collins toward the Moon. The Apollo 11 mission used the same Saturn V rocket that Wernher von Braun had developed for earlier Apollo missions. On July 20, 1969, Neil Armstrong became the first human to walk on the Moon, followed shortly afterward by Buzz Aldrin, while Michael Collins remained in lunar orbit aboard the command module Columbia. Armstrong's famous words, "That's one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind," were broadcast live to an estimated worldwide television audience of hundreds of millions of people. The lunar module used for the landing, named Eagle, had been developed at NASA's Manned Spacecraft Center, the same facility that had managed Project Gemini. Apollo 11 fulfilled the goal that President John F. Kennedy had set before Congress in 1961, ending the Space Race's lunar phase with an American victory. Six more Apollo missions followed, with Apollo 17 in 1972 marking the final crewed Moon landing of the twentieth century.
