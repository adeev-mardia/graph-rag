Wernher von Braun's work on the V-2 rocket in Nazi Germany during the Second World War made him the most important rocket engineer to be recruited by the United States after the war, under a program called Operation Paperclip. Von Braun first worked for the US Army developing the Redstone rocket, which later launched Alan Shepard on the first American human spaceflight during Project Mercury. After NASA was created in 1958, von Braun moved to the newly formed Marshall Space Flight Center, where he led the team that designed the Saturn V rocket used throughout the Apollo program. Von Braun's Saturn V design was so capable that leftover Saturn V hardware was later repurposed to launch the Skylab space station in 1973. Von Braun died in 1977, a few years after the Apollo program ended, having seen the rocket he designed carry twenty-four astronauts toward the Moon. Decades later, NASA's Space Launch System rocket for the Artemis program drew on structural and propulsion lessons first learned during the Saturn V program that von Braun had led.
