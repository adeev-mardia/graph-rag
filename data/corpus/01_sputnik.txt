The Space Race began in earnest on October 4, 1957, when the Soviet Union launched Sputnik 1 into low Earth orbit. Sputnik 1 was the first artificial satellite ever placed into orbit, and its success stunned the United States government. The satellite was designed under the direction of Sergei Korolev, the chief rocket engineer of the Soviet space program. Korolev had spent years developing the R-7 rocket, which carried Sputnik 1 into space from the Baikonur Cosmodrome in Kazakhstan. The launch of Sputnik 1 triggered what historians now call the Sputnik Crisis in the United States, prompting Congress to increase funding for science education. In response to Sputnik 1, President Dwight Eisenhower approved the creation of the National Aeronautics and Space Administration, known as NASA, in 1958. NASA was formed from the earlier National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics and absorbed several military rocket research programs. The Soviet Union followed Sputnik 1 with Sputnik 2, which carried a dog named Laika into orbit, making Laika the first living creature to orbit the Earth. Korolev also oversaw the development of the Vostok program, which would later carry the first human into space. The early success of the Soviet space program under Korolev gave the Soviet Union a significant lead in the opening years of the Space Race.
