After the Apollo program ended, NASA launched Skylab in 1973, the first American space station, using leftover hardware from the Saturn V rocket program. Skylab hosted three crews of astronauts who conducted scientific experiments in microgravity before the station was deliberately deorbited in 1979. The Soviet Union pursued its own space station program with Salyut and later Mir, which remained in orbit from 1986 until it was deorbited in 2001. Mir hosted numerous international crews, including American astronauts, during a period of cooperation between NASA and the Russian space agency Roscosmos in the 1990s. This cooperation on Mir laid the groundwork for the International Space Station, a joint project between NASA, Roscosmos, the European Space Agency, the Japanese Aerospace Exploration Agency, and the Canadian Space Agency. The International Space Station's first module was launched in 1998, and it has been continuously occupied by rotating international crews since November 2000. Astronauts travel to the International Space Station aboard Russian Soyuz spacecraft and, since 2020, aboard American SpaceX Crew Dragon capsules.
