On April 12, 1961, Yuri Gagarin became the first human to travel into outer space aboard the Vostok 1 spacecraft. Vostok 1 completed a single orbit of the Earth before Gagarin ejected and parachuted safely back to the ground near the village of Smelovka in the Soviet Union. The Vostok program that carried Gagarin into orbit had been developed by Sergei Korolev and his design bureau, building on the same rocket engineering that had launched Sputnik 1 four years earlier. Gagarin's flight was a major propaganda victory for the Soviet Union during the Cold War and intensified pressure on the United States to respond. Shortly after Gagarin's flight, President John F. Kennedy announced before Congress that the United States would commit to landing a man on the Moon before the end of the decade. This announcement launched the Apollo program at NASA. Valentina Tereshkova, who later flew aboard Vostok 6 in 1963, became the first woman in space, continuing the legacy of the Vostok program that Korolev had designed. Gagarin remained a celebrated figure in the Soviet Union until his death in a jet training accident in 1968.
