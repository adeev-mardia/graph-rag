The Indian Space Research Organisation, known as ISRO, launched Chandrayaan-1 in 2008 as India's first mission to the Moon, carrying instruments that helped confirm the presence of water molecules on the lunar surface. ISRO followed this success with Chandrayaan-2 in 2019, which included an orbiter, a lander named Vikram, and a rover named Pragyan, although the lander crashed during its final descent. In August 2023, ISRO's Chandrayaan-3 mission successfully landed the Vikram lander near the Moon's south pole, making India the fourth country to achieve a soft lunar landing after the Soviet Union, the United States, and China. ISRO's success with Chandrayaan-3 came shortly after Russia's Luna-25 mission, a successor to the Soviet-era Luna program once overseen by Sergei Korolev's design bureau, crashed while attempting a similar landing near the lunar south pole. ISRO has stated that data from Chandrayaan-1's discovery of lunar water influenced NASA's planning for the Artemis program's targeted landing sites near the Moon's south pole.
