In response to Soviet advances in human spaceflight, NASA established Project Mercury in 1958 as its first human spaceflight program. Alan Shepard became the first American in space in May 1961 aboard the Mercury spacecraft Freedom 7, a few weeks after Yuri Gagarin's orbital flight for the Soviet Union. John Glenn later became the first American to orbit the Earth in February 1962, flying aboard the Mercury capsule Friendship 7. After Project Mercury concluded, NASA began Project Gemini in 1961 to develop the techniques needed for a Moon landing, including orbital rendezvous and spacewalks. Astronaut Ed White performed the first American spacewalk during the Gemini 4 mission in 1965. The Gemini program was managed out of NASA's Manned Spacecraft Center in Houston, Texas, which would later be renamed the Johnson Space Center. Many of the astronauts who flew during Project Gemini, including Neil Armstrong and Jim Lovell, went on to fly in the later Apollo program. Project Gemini proved that astronauts could dock two spacecraft together in orbit, a skill that would be essential for the Apollo Moon landings.
